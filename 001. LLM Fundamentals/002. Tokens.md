Tokens are like the 'currency' of the LLMs.

At the most fundamental level, a token is the basic unit of text that an LLM processes. It is important to understand that LLMs do not read raw text like humans do. They operate on 'tokens' which are converted into numerical IDs and then processed by the model.

A common misconception is that tokens are words. That is incorrect. Tokens are typically sub-word units, meaning a single word can be split into multiple tokens.

Let's take some examples -

    "hello" → ["hello"]          (1 token)
    "unbelievable" → ["un", "believ", "able"]   (3 tokens)
    "ChatGPT" → ["Chat", "G", "PT"]
    "I love coding" → ["I", " love", " coding"]

Notice that space may be part of tokens.

Moreover, the 'rare' or 'complex' words get split more.

Now you might think, why not just use words directly? That's because using full words would need us to have a huge vocabulary or millions of words. This would also mean poor handling or new or rare words and eventually, inefficient learning.

Tokenization strikes a balance -

    1. Smaller vocabulary
    2. Reusable building blocks
    3. Better generalization

# HOW TOKENIZATION WORKS?

Tokenization is handles by various algorithms like BPE (Byte Pair Encoding), WordPiece etc. These algorithms start with characters, merge frequently occurring patterns and then build a vocabulary of subword units.

Before entering the model, tokens are converted into token IDs. For example -

    "I love coding"
    ↓
    ["I", " love", " coding"]
    ↓
    [101, 2345, 9982]   (example IDs)

These IDs are then mapped to vectors (embeddings) inside the model.

The process of converting text into tokens is called 'Encoding' where the tokenizer splits the text into tokens it recognizes and then converts the tokens into numbers.

Decoding is the reverse process where the numbers are converted back into text tokens and the tokens are joined to form the output.

# VOCABULARY SIZE MATTERS

The vocabulary is the set of all tokens a model knows. So, if a model has a vocabulary size of 50,000, it means that there are 50,000 unique tokens and every input/output token must come from this set.

The size of the vocabulary matters because it controls how the text is split. A large vocabulary means the whole words are stored as single tokens so fewer splits so fewer tokens.

    "unbelievable" → ["unbelievable"]

A smaller vocabulary means more aggressive splitting and hence longer token sequences.

    "unbelievable" → ["un", "believ", "able"]

So, small vocabularies means more tokens per sentence while larger vocabularies means fewer tokens per sentence. And more the tokens, more compute is required.

Now, vocabulary size also affects model size. Larger vocabulary means bigger model.

Finally, remember that Vocabulary size is not a primary driver of intelligence.

A model with -

    50K vocabulary + strong architecture + good training

can outperform the model with -

    200K vocabulary + weak training

# UNUSUAL WORDS

When a word is not common enough to exist as a single token, the tokenizer breaks it into smaller, more frequent pieces.

For example -

    "antidisestablishmentarianism"
    → ["anti", "dis", "establish", "ment", "arian", "ism"]

Here, each piece is a known token. The model processes the sequence and reconstructs meaning from parts.

During training, the model learns patterns at multiple granularities -

    prefixes (un, anti, re)
    roots (form, struct, code)
    suffixes (ing, tion, ism)

So even if a full word is rare, its components are not. This gives the model a form of compositional understanding.

If a word is truly novel:

    "flibbertzorp"

It may be tokenized into smaller fragments:

    ["fl", "ib", "bert", "z", "orp"]

At this point, the model has no direct meaning stored and it relies on context to infer meaning.
